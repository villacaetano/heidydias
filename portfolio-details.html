Project / Paper Overview

An iterative approach to optimise deep-sea biotope classification using a combination of acoustic data and Remotely Operated Vehicle (ROV) video footage was developed and tested at the Tropic Seamount site in the Northeast Atlantic.

Two methods for biotope classification were compared:

  • Top-down approach – based on acoustic substrate classification followed by biological characterisation
  • Bottom-up approach – using multivariate analysis of biological assemblages only

Video transects were analysed at two spatial resolutions (200 m and 50 m segments) to assess scale effects on biotope delineation. The bottom-up approach using 50 m segments identified 12 distinct biotopes with stronger environmental correlations compared to broader classifications at 200 m scale.

This study demonstrates that shorter transects (50 m) combined with bottom-up sampling approaches are preferable for capturing the ecological heterogeneity characteristic of deep-sea seamount environments, with important implications for vulnerable marine ecosystem identification and spatial management.

Key Contributions

Comparative Methodology

Direct comparison of top-down vs bottom-up biotope classification approaches in a deep-sea seamount setting.

Scale Effects

Demonstrated that 50 m video segments capture ecological heterogeneity better than 200 m segments.

12 Distinct Biotopes

Identified 12 distinct biotopes on Tropic Seamount using the bottom-up 50 m approach.

Management Relevance

Provides practical insights for vulnerable marine ecosystem (VME) identification and spatial management.
